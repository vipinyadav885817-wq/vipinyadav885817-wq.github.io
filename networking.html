1. How the Internet & Networks Work

Learn the basics of LAN, WAN, routers, and switches, and how your device connects to servers across the world.

+

What is a Network?

A computer network is simply two or more devices connected together to share data and resources. Whether you are sending a message or loading a website, your data travels across multiple connected devices.

Key Network Components

  • LAN (Local Area Network): A small network within a single building, like your home Wi-Fi or an office network.
  • WAN (Wide Area Network): A large network spanning cities or countries. The Internet is the largest WAN in the world.
  • Switch: Connects multiple devices inside a single local network (LAN) and sends data only to the intended device.
  • Router: Connects different networks together (for example, connecting your home LAN to the Internet).
Security Takeaway: Always change the default admin password on your home router, as it is the main gateway between your devices and the public internet.

2. IP Addresses & MAC Addresses

Discover the difference between physical hardware addresses (MAC) and logical network addresses (IPv4 & IPv6).

+

Two Types of Addresses

To send data to the right destination, networks rely on two distinct addressing systems working together:

  • MAC Address (Physical): A permanent, unique identifier burned into your device's network card (e.g., 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E). Used inside local networks.
  • IP Address (Logical): A temporary address assigned to your device when it joins a network (e.g., 192.168.1.15 for IPv4). It changes when you switch Wi-Fi networks.

Public vs. Private IP

Your Private IP identifies your device inside your home network, while your Public IP is assigned by your Internet Service Provider (ISP) and is visible to the outside world.
